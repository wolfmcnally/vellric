The primary described a second code-advice pass as required when that obligation arose from the fresh full evidence run it had selected. The recipient's primary-mode rules already allowed later corrections to be assessed and tested without a mandatory additional review; its older high-risk follow-up routing also differed from the newer upstream operator-choice ruling. The actual second pass completed within its ordinary allowance, but that does not make the earlier requirement claim accurate.

Before calling a review mandatory, distinguish the active product-policy requirement, the operator's explicit route, the primary's discretionary risk judgment and the mechanical requirements of a container the primary chose. Read the actual recipient policy and dated adoption evidence; a newer upstream rule is not automatically adopted, and an old recipient rule is not evidence that the operator requested a phase. A permitted unused slot is a ceiling, not a reason to launch. Record a chosen second pass as a choice with its concrete cause, preserve consumed history, and do not create an avoidable review dependency merely to satisfy a self-selected evidence route.

The correction recorded the exact active/upstream clauses, the limited last-teach provenance, the completed second pass and the overstatement without refunding or hiding it. No policy migration, review waiver or additional invocation followed from the comparison. This is an unsettled lesson; only the operator may graduate it.
